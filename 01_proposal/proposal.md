# Data Management and Visualization Specialization Project

## Topic

I want to explore the relationship between **educational attainment** and
**political ideology**. Education shapes how people engage with politics,
form ideological views, and participate in civic life, making it a
widely relevant demographic factor to examine. For this project I'm using
the **[Outlook on Life Survey](https://d396qusza40orc.cloudfront.net/phoenixassets/data-management-visualization/OutlookOnLife%20Codebook.pdf)**
and its codebook (ICPSR 35348, Robnett & Tate, 2012).

## Data Set

I selected information about respondents' educational attainment and
their ideological self-placement. I'm interested in examining whether a
person's level of education is associated with where they place
themselves on the liberal–conservative spectrum.

## My Personal Codebook

**Educational attainment**

- `PPEDUC` — Education (Highest Degree Received)
- `PPEDUCAT` — Education (Categorical: Less than HS, High School, Some
  College, Bachelor's or higher)

**Political identity / ideology**

- `W1_C2` — Ideological self-placement, 7-point scale (Extremely liberal
  → Extremely conservative)

My two primary constructs are therefore:

**Construct 1:** Educational attainment
**Construct 2:** Ideological self-placement

## Literature Review

* I used google scholar and a few search terms used include : "education and political
ideology", "education and political identity", "diploma divide
ideology", "higher education liberal conservative attitudes"*

- Le, K., & Nguyen, M. (2021) . *Education and political engagement. *International Journal of Educational Development.— This study contributes to literature by investigating educational attainment in relation to political engagement in individuals and reached the following findings. Education is positively associated with individuals interest in politics and self-perceived
interest in politics. Completing one more year of education raises the individual’s interest in politics by 6.7 % and increases the individual’s self-perceived knowledge of politics by 8.5 %. Second, better educated  individuals are more inclined to possess supportive attitudes towards political freedoms. An additional year of education raises the probability of
the individual endorsing the organization of public meetings and marches-demonstrations to protest against the government by 6 and 8 %, respectively. 

- Scott, R. (2022) . *Does university make you more liberal? Estimating the within-individual effects of higher education on political values. * Electoral Studies.- This study examines whether university education causally affects political values, addressing the selection bias problem where certain types of people choose higher education.
Using longitudinal data from the 1970 British Cohort Study, the research tracks individuals over time to estimate within-person changes in political attitudes. The study finds that achieving a university degree causes three significant changes in political values, namely it reduces racial prejudice, decreases authoritarianism and increases economically right wing attitudes. 

- Kenworthy, L., & Malami, M. (1999). *Gender inequality in political representation: A worldwide comparative analysis. * Social forces.- The paper finds that women’s political representation varies across countries because of a combination of political, socioeconomic, and cultural factors. Electoral systems matter, systems that make it easier for parties to nominate women
tend to have more women in parliament. Left-wing governments are associated with greater representation of women. Countries where women gained voting rights earlier tend to have higher levels of representation. A higher share of women in professional occupations is linked to greater political representation. Cultural attitudes toward women in politics also have a significant
effect. Overall, no single factor explains gender inequality; political institutions, women’s socioeconomic position, and cultural norms all contribute.

- Prinzing, M., & Vazquez, M. (2026). *larifying the diploma divide: The growing importance of higher education for political identity. * Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.- Investigates the relationship between higher education (educational attainment) and political identity, referred to as the “diploma divide,” by analyzing changes in ideological views on social
- and economic issues among US adults over the past half-century and tracking changes in political identity during students' time in higher education. Adults with more education have consistently held more left-leaning views on social issues, but this effect has not been observed for economic issues, suggesting a split influence of educational attainment on different aspects of
- ideology. Before the 2010s, there were no meaningful educational differences in the degree to which people identified as liberal versus conservative, but in the years since, college graduates have increasingly identified as liberal while those with some or no college education remained steady.

  
## Research Question

Is educational attainment associated with ideological self-placement?

## Hypothesis

Educational attainment will be significantly associated with
ideological self-placement, such that individuals with different levels
of education will differ in how liberal or conservative they identify.

**More specific hypothesis:** Individuals with higher levels of
educational attainment will be more likely to identify as liberal, while
individuals with lower levels of educational attainment will be more
likely to identify as conservative or moderate.
